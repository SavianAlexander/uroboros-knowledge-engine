Fanfestive capsuleers, 

Once again, capsuleers from across the galaxy will converge on Reykjavik from 14-16 May, as EVE Fanfest returns. 

As ever, Fanfest is a celebration of the EVE community. From long-time alliances to new pilots, content creators to developers, everyone who helps shape New Eden has a place here. 

Across multiple days and nights, the Harpa Conference & Concert Hall will be alive with presentations, hands-on experiences, community spaces, and unforgettable moments both on and off the stage. 

Beyond Harpa, Reykjavik itself becomes part of the experience as capsuleers take over the city with meetups, events, and late-night conversations. 

This post will act as your central source of information on everything happening at EVE Fanfest 2026. Whether you’re attending in person or joining remotely, you’ll find all the key information right here, to help you plan your time and make the most of the festival.

### The Capsuleer Edda


Fanfest guests will have a chance to participate in one of the boldest cultural projects ever undertaken in EVE: the Capsuleer Edda. Rooted in Icelandic manuscript tradition and built to preserve the living history of New Eden in physical form, this extraordinary work brings together epic poetry, calligraphy, vellum, and symbolic capsuleer participation. 

Guests at Fanfest will be able to contribute a single drop of blood to the project, becoming part of a manuscript designed to endure for millennia. Note: this will not affect your standings with the Blood Raiders.

While digital worlds can evolve and disappear, or become inaccessible, vellum manuscripts have endured for centuries or even millennia, which is why that’s the medium chosen to preserve the living history of New Eden. 

**World in Words – a Preview Exhibition from Árnastofnun
**While waiting to donate a drop of their precious bodily fluids to the Capsuleer Edda, attendees will get the rare chance to see an Icelandic vellum manuscript with their own eyes, and steep themselves in the world that inspired the Capsuleer Edda by learning more about Iceland’s manuscript tradition and the legacy of preserving stories in physical form.

### Iceland Intel & Navigation Guide


If this is your first visit to Iceland, here’s a quick rundown. 


- **Weather** 
The forecast for Fanfest is always uncertain. When in doubt, expect the classic Icelandic “four seasons per day” situation, where it may be sunny, then rainy, then windy, then all of the above. As they saying goes, if you don’t like the weather, wait five minutes. Temperatures usually hover around 5-12°C (40-44°F), with a mix of sun, wind, and the occasional rain shower. Bring layers and a waterproof jacket. You’ll thank yourself later. Umbrellas don’t normally do well with the harsh winds, so bring them at your own peril.
- **Language** 
The local language is Icelandic, but everyone speaks excellent English. You’ll have no problem getting around, and menus and important signs will almost always have translations. When in doubt, don’t hesitate to ask!
- **Money** 
Iceland’s currency is the ISK, but sadly your in-game wallets won’t work. Icelandic society is all but cashless, so credit/debit cards (as well as Apple/Google Pay) are accepted everywhere, from bars to busses to hot dog stands. Be sure to notify your credit card company before you travel to let them know. If you want to have cash on hand, the best exchange rates tend to be at the airport upon arrival.
- **Connectivity** 
WiFi surfing is easy! Harpa, every hotel, and most restaurants offer free WiFi access. However, if you want a local data plan, grabbing an eSIM is also quick and easy. [Nova offers one](https://portal.nova.is/farsimi/eSIM_iceland) you can set up in advance or on arrival.
- **Airport transfers** 
Keflavik International Airport (KEF) is about 45 minutes from Reykjavik. The simplest option is [Flybus](https://www.flybus.is), which runs in sync with flights and drops you off at major hotels or the BSI bus terminal.


Although Reykjavik is small for a capital, getting around can take up precious time you could otherwise spend at a meetup! Since Harpa is centrally located, most places are within walking distance, but if you’re looking to get from A to B a bit faster, here are a few options:


- [Public bus service](https://visitreykjavik.is/public-transportation)
- [Hopp electric scooters](https://hopp.bike/)
- [Hopp rideshare](https://hopp.bike/car-sharing)
- [Hreyfill Taxi](https://www.hreyfill.is/en/)
